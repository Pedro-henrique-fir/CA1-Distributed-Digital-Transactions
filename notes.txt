In a normal hotel booking, the customer depends on other parties for almost everything. The hotel keeps the booking records and decides about refunds, and the payment usually goes through a booking website or a bank before it reaches the hotel. So the customer cannot really check what is happening and has to trust everyone in the middle. My idea is to use the blockchain as the middle layer between the hotel and the guest. In simple terms, the smart contract keeps the records, holds the money and follows the rules automatically.

Trust and transparency: every booking is saved on the blockchain, so the guest and the hotel can both check it at any time.

Refunds: if the guest cancels before the deadline, the contract sends the refund automatically, without waiting for the hotel to decide.

Payment intermediaries: the money stays in the contract, like an escrow and go to the hotel only when the check in is confirmed. No bank or booking website needs to hold it.


Use Cases

Hotel: register rooms, set the room price, view reservations and receive payments

Guest: check available rooms, book a room and pay, view a reservation, cancel a booking before the deadline and get the money back when eligible

Rules:

Only the hotel owner can add rooms and set their prices
Each booking is for one room and one night
Two guests cannot book the same room for the same date
The guest must pay the exact price to confirm a booking
The payment stays in the smart contract until it is refunded or released to the hotel
Only the guest who made the booking can cancel it
If the guest cancels at least 24 hours before check-in, they receive a full refund, but after this deadline cancellation is not allowed
Once a booking is confirmed, its price and details cannot be changed
The hotel can withdraw the payment after the check-in time
Each booking has a status like confirmed, cancelled or completed
The same payment can not be refunded or withdrawn more than once




Data

For the rooms the smart contract need to store the room number, the price per night and whether the room is available or not.
For each booking, it need to keep a booking id, the room number, the guest wallet address, the check-in date, the amount paid and the booking status.

The contract also needs to know which rooms are already booked for each date to avoid double bookings.

For personal information like names and emails, I would keep this in the hotel private system instead of putting it on the blockchain. The idea is to connect both systems using the booking id, so the hotel can identify the guest when they arrive.

Integration with the Hotel System

When a guest makes a booking, the smart contract checks the availability, receives the payment and records the reservation on the blockchain.

After the transaction is confirmed, the hotel system can read the booking information and link it to the guest personal details stored privately.

This way, the blockchain handles the booking rules, payments and refunds, while the hotel still manages guest information and the check-in process.


Cryptography


My system has four security needs: authentication, non-repudiation, integrity and confidentiality. I chose ECDSA (Elliptic Curve Digital Signature Algorithm), the method Ethereum already uses, because it covers most of them.

Authentication: only the guest who made a booking can cancel it, and only the hotel can withdraw the payment. Every transaction is signed with the sender’s private key, and the signature proves which wallet sent it (Ethereum Community, 2026b). The private key cannot be found from the public key (Ethereum Community, 2026b), so nobody can cancel or withdraw in someone else’s name.

Non-repudiation: because every booking is signed, the guest cannot later deny that they booked or cancelled. This helps if there is a dispute.

Integrity: Ethereum stores data with Keccak-256 hashes. If anyone changes a stored booking, the root hash changes and the change is detected (Ethereum Community, 2026c).

Confidentiality: ECDSA does not encrypt data, and the blockchain is public. So the contract only stores the wallet address, room, date and amount. Names and emails stay in the hotel’s private system, linked by the booking id.

Why ECDSA?

First, the system needs asymmetric cryptography. With a symmetric method, like AES, the guest and the hotel would use the same secret key, so it would be impossible to prove which of them signed a transaction. With ECDSA, only the private key can sign, but anyone can check the signature with the public key, which proves who wrote the message (Ethereum Community, 2026b).

Second, ECDSA is already built into Ethereum. Every wallet uses it. RSA could also sign data, but it would need a second key system on top of the wallet, which adds complexity.


Benefits

Less dependence on intermediaries

    Today the payment passes through booking websites and banks before it reaches the hotel. In my system, the contract holds the payment and applies the rules, so the hotel can sell directly to the guest. This is the disruptive part, because it replaces a job that booking platforms do today.

Same rules for everyone

    refunds and payments happen automatically, based on dates that both sides can see. The hotel cannot refuse a refund that the rules allow, and the guest cannot cancel after the deadline, so there is less room for disputes.

Shared record

    the guest and the hotel see the same booking history, and nobody can change it later. If there is a disagreement, both sides can check the same data.

Challenges

Real world events
    the contract cannot know if the guest really arrived. Smart contracts cannot access information outside the blockchain by themselves (Ethereum Community, 2026d), so in my system the hotel is still trusted for the check-in. This means blockchain reduces trust in the middle, but it does not remove it completely.

Personal data
    as a general rule, storing personal data on a blockchain should be avoided (EDPB, 2025). Even a wallet address could be linked to a person, so my contract stores as little as possible and keeps names and emails out of the blockchain.

Costs and adoption
    every transaction costs gas, the price of ETH changes a lot, and many guests do not have a crypto wallet. For a normal guest, this can be harder than paying with a card.

Recent Advancements

Cheaper transactions

    rollups group many transactions together off-chain, and since the Dencun upgrade in March 2024 they use cheaper “blob” storage (Ethereum Community, 2026e). This makes small payments like a hotel booking more realistic.

Stable prices

    stablecoins are tokens designed to keep a fixed value, even when the price of ETH changes (Ethereum Community, 2026f). The hotel could charge in a stablecoin, so the room price stays the same.

Easier wallets

    with account abstraction, applications can pay the gas fees for their users (Ethereum Community, 2026a). So the hotel could pay the gas, and the guest would not need to own ETH to book.
